\begin{abstract}
MINTS audits the gap between label decodability, attention association and causal use in DNABERT-2. Six of ten historical TATA patching pairs fail nucleotide correspondence, and unstable restoration ratios weaken their interpretation. In an accessibility-conditioned CTCF comparison, the fitted baseline-plus-residual classifier performs worse than the motif, position and composition baseline; this does not show that residual representations contain no additional information. Repaired assays enforce nucleotide correspondence, composition-preserving motif edits and matched shams, with discovery-selected heads and chromosome-held-out confirmation. The CTCF trained-readout intervention covers 64 sequences but only 45 genomic clusters, and does not establish native pretrained binding causality. A separate fixed-endpoint native masked-flank pilot retains 12 discovery sequences in 11 clusters but fails its motif-minus-sham sensitivity gate, so head search and fresh confirmation stop. Engineered attention fixtures calibrate specific single-head and distributed mechanisms; individual-head screen failures do not exclude distributed genomic mechanisms. The audit supports bounded computational claims and leaves biological binding causality unresolved.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
A motif is a recurring short DNA pattern associated with a biological process. A genomic transformer can classify DNA containing such a pattern without assigning that computation to one attention head. A linear probe may recover the label from frozen hidden states, while attention maps may emphasize nearby bases. Neither observation shows that the head detects the motif or that the model uses its output for a biological prediction.

We ask what evidence would justify that stronger component-level claim. The distinction between the query--key computation that directs attention and the output--value computation that writes information motivates separate tests of localization and downstream effect \citep{elhage2021framework}. Activation patching contributes intervention evidence only after the output, corruption, and component being exchanged have been specified \citep{heimersheim2024patching}. Our contribution is an explicit standard for reporting these different forms of evidence, together with an audit of a DNABERT-2 case study \citep{zhou2024dnabert2}.

CTCF is a protein that binds DNA and contributes to chromosome organization. A CTCF peak is a genomic interval enriched in a binding assay; a peak need not contain a high-scoring match to one motif matrix. We use the curated JASPAR MA0139.1 matrix to define a testable motif-local hypothesis. Promoters regulate transcription near gene starts, and a TATA box is one promoter-associated A/T-rich pattern. Splice donor and acceptor signals mark boundaries removed when RNA is processed. These auxiliary classification tasks test representation decodability, rather than substituting for a CTCF-specific causal experiment.

The evidence standard is a reporting discipline, not an exhaustive definition of every possible motif detector. A distributed detector, a detector active at particular queries, or a detector expressed through nonlinear interactions can fail our screens. We distinguish failure to pass a specified assay from absence of a biological mechanism.

\paragraph{Relation to prior work.}
DNABERT already uses attention to identify conserved sequence motifs \citep{ji2021dnabert}. We do not claim to introduce attention-based motif discovery. Zhang and Nanda show that corruption and metric choices can change activation-patching conclusions \citep{zhang2024metrics}; our composition-preserving edits, matched shams and absolute endpoints test those concerns under variable-width DNA tokenization. OmniGenBench addresses reproducible genomic model evaluation and interoperability \citep{yang2025omnigenbench}. MINTS adds a narrower empirical assay audit, known-mechanism computational calibration, and frozen-head confirmation for specified trained outputs. The synthetic fixtures do not prove assay sensitivity to every learned genomic mechanism.

Ali's recent sparse-dictionary study compares native prediction-distribution shifts after feature ablation in binding-associated populations \citep{ali2026causal}. Our unchanged masked-token endpoint separates a specified sequence-prediction effect from biological binding causality; allele-specific binding measurements remain an independent requirement.

\paragraph{Predictive comparisons.}
The encoder is \texttt{zhihan1996/DNABERT-2-117M}, which uses variable-length byte-pair tokens. We mean-pool non-padding hidden states, including special tokens, at zero-based encoder layer 11, then train a standardized, class-balanced logistic classifier. The earlier labels ``Seq-head'' and ``Readout'' described this same classifier, so we report it once as a frozen readout. No encoder fine-tuning is performed.

The composition baseline uses GC fraction, GC skew, and sequence length. GC denotes the fraction of valid bases that are G or C. The sequence baseline uses TF-IDF counts of overlapping character 3--6-mers and balanced logistic regression. This range covers short local patterns at manageable feature cost; it was a heuristic configuration, not an optimum chosen using an independent validation set. TF-IDF is fit on training sequences only, with minimum document frequency 2 and at most 50,000 features. Solver settings, seeds, and predictions are saved with the regenerated artifacts.

Historical nearest-GC matching without a distance limit merely reorders both balanced promoter test sets. We replace it with matching without replacement within 0.02 GC fraction, introduced heuristically during this revision, and report the retained population. This changes the estimand to sequences with composition overlap, and does not eliminate other confounding features.

\paragraph{Uncertainty and selection.}
Predictive intervals are percentile intervals from 1,000 resamples of test sequences, seed 1729, conditional on a fixed trained classifier. They exclude training-set and checkpoint variability. Undefined single-class draws are omitted for AUROC and AUPRC. Sequences, rather than their individual tokens, are resampled. Exploratory head-level intervals are marginal; choosing the largest effect among 144 heads does not make its interval evidence of discovery. Confirmatory head searches require sequence-level null inference with correction across all tested heads, such as Holm family-wise correction. Historical token-level Pearson p-values are not used for significance claims here.

\paragraph{Nucleotide-to-token alignment.}
A position weight matrix gives a nucleotide preference at each motif position. We normalize MA0139.1 counts with pseudocount 0.5 and scan both orientations with log-odds scores. The historical support threshold is $s_{\min}+0.8(s_{\max}-s_{\min})$. A hit spans $[a,b)$ and a token spans $[u,v)$. It belongs to motif support when $\max(0,\min(v,b)-\max(u,a))\geq1$. Zero-width special tokens remain aligned to hidden states but receive no support. Each token's score is the maximum over overlapping scored motif windows.

\paragraph{Historical CTCF assay.}
For every head, the assay averages bilinear QK scores over all non-padding queries, including special tokens, and correlates resulting key scores with finite motif scores pooled across sequences. Its attention map is reconstructed from QK weight factors. Equivalence to native attention, including projection biases, positional terms, and normalization, has not been established. Enrichment therefore describes the implemented reconstruction.

Background positions are the nearest token indices outside each individual motif-hit interval, matching token count. They are not matched for GC, nucleotide span, or accessibility, and can overlap other hits or special tokens. Enrichment is the ratio of aggregate attention mass per motif-hit token occurrence to mass per background occurrence, summing over queries. Longer sequences and overlapping hits affect this historical weighting. This ratio, sometimes denoted $\rho$, is not Spearman correlation and can exceed one.

\paragraph{Native-attention genomic controls.}
We separately match motif-absent peaks without replacement to motif-present peaks on chromosome, GC fraction within 0.02, and nucleotide length within 5\%. The first connected motif-support span in the present sequence is projected to the same relative position in the absent sequence. These are PWM-absent binding peaks, not unbound genomic regions; accessibility and other sequence features remain possible confounders.

For checkpoint revision \path{7bce263b15377fc15361f52cfab88f8b586abda0}, attention reconstruction includes projection biases and the native positional bias and mask. We check reconstructed attention times native values against every native head-context output. Queries and target keys span nucleotides and exclude special tokens; softmax still includes native special-token keys. The score averages attention over real queries and target keys, multiplied by the number of real keys to normalize uniform attention density. Each matched sequence pair is one unit. We use 1,000 pair-bootstrap draws for marginal intervals and 9,999 paired sign flips for two-sided tests, with Holm correction over 144 heads. Conditional pair exchangeability is an observational assumption, and within-chromosome dependence is not modeled. These new matching and inference settings were chosen during revision.

\paragraph{Sequence-level content QK.}
For the same matched population, we average scaled content QK logits over nucleotide queries, including projection biases but excluding the ALiBi positional term. Within each sequence, we correlate these key scores with finite continuous PWM scores over nucleotide keys. Each sequence contributes one correlation, irrespective of its token count. Constant-score or insufficient-score sequences have undefined correlations; pairs containing any undefined head are excluded from QK inference and counted separately. We bootstrap matched pairs for mean motif-present correlations and present--absent contrasts, and apply the same sign-flip design to contrasts with a separate Holm family of 144 heads. These tests assess a conditional contrast between peaks, not a token-level null or a causal detector.

\paragraph{Historical patching procedure, retained as audit.}
We replace the first TATA-like pattern matching \texttt{TATA[AT]A} with a deterministic G/C-rich sequence of the same nucleotide length. This changes composition as well as motif identity. We retain positive held-out examples whose clean and corrupted token counts agree; equal counts alone do not establish identical nucleotide boundaries. At one layer and head, we insert the clean pre-output-projection head context into the corrupted run. The geometric sparse selector retains motif-overlapping query blocks, their selected key blocks, and sequence endpoints, with a position budget of $\max(8,4\lceil\log_2 N\rceil)$ for $N$ tokens. It uses the edited span rather than measured attention weights; if span alignment fails, it falls back to the central token. This position rule is part of the intervention and does not validate a localization mechanism. The scalar is the trained standardized logistic probe's decision function on the mean-pooled layer-11 residual vector, not a pretrained native output.
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{PM}=\frac{f(x_{\mathrm{patched}})-f(x_{\mathrm{corrupted}})}{f(x_{\mathrm{clean}})-f(x_{\mathrm{corrupted}})}.
\end{equation}
Near-zero denominators, absolute effects, and denominator signs must be examined alongside PM. Values above one indicate overshoot and can reflect off-manifold combinations or denominator sensitivity. Clean/corrupted scores, pair effects, IDs, medians, and bootstrap intervals are saved. Patching intervals resample exact clean-sequence clusters, retaining all locus pairs within a sampled cluster; reported means and medians remain locus-weighted. The TATA inputs are unique. No native CTCF activation-patching result is claimed because a validated native CTCF output is not defined.

\section{Results}
\paragraph{The audit separates evidence that supports different claims.}
Decodable labels and conditional attention associations do not establish causal motif use. Historical patching has broken nucleotide alignment and unstable restoration ratios. The fitted CTCF baseline-plus-residual classifier has a negative incremental comparison on the analyzed population; it does not prove absence of additional representational information. The repaired trained-readout intervention uses 64 sequences in 45 genomic clusters. Its target remains a fitted readout, so these measurements cannot establish a native biological binding mechanism. Individual-head screen failures also leave distributed mechanisms unresolved.

\section{Interpretation and limitations}
The native extension is gated on endpoint feasibility. Its current masked-flank pilot fails that gate, and its interval is too wide for a precise null. Any revised endpoint is exploratory. A subsequent confirmation would require a fully frozen endpoint, selection and intervention rule, matched controls, eligibility, genomic clustering and a cluster-based power justification on genuinely uninspected inputs. The inspected chromosomes 20/21 cannot provide that confirmation. GSE81945 is a candidate independent allele-specific binding source, but its listed processed peaks use hg19 and do not provide a ready paired variant-effect table; usable allele sample size and genome-build compatibility remain unverified. Natural substitutions require substitution-matched controls, rather than composition-preserving shuffle controls.
Evidence supports frozen decodability, failure of historical CTCF screens, and sequence-level QK and native-attention measurements under matched genomic controls, without identifying native CTCF causality. A validated native pretrained causal target remains unresolved; the added CTCF target is an independently evaluated trained readout. The corrected analyses use different aggregation and a different control population from the historical screens; the assays are not interchangeable. Small selected patching populations and composition-changing corruptions prevent broad causal conclusions. The controlled assays above establish nucleotide correspondence and matched shams for their retained populations, with explicit eligibility exclusions.

The affine QK observation is conditional: if logits for a fixed query equal $a+\gamma s_j$ with $\gamma>0$, softmax preserves score ordering within that sequence. This assumption is not shown empirically. Within-sequence ranking does not imply a cross-sequence motif-present/absent threshold or causal use; we make no stronger layer-1 theorem claim.

The Nucleotide Transformer comparison is excluded because a pinned checkpoint, validated token alignment, and native-attention equivalence were not established. Its stored enrichment ratio is not Spearman correlation. No tokenizer-family ranking or cross-model biological conclusion is supported. Auxiliary OV and sparse-feature artifacts are historical; weak alignment does not show that an alternate circuit is absent.

Intervals concern fixed classifiers and do not resolve labeling errors, pretraining contamination, distant homology or training-seed variability. The added analyses report sensitivity to within-chromosome dependence through transitive genomic blocks. Local membership matches the pinned upstream release, but historical checkpoint provenance and independent label reconstruction remain incomplete. The predictive, held-out TATA, and native-control results use the completed clean CUDA reproduction. Fresh train/test activation caches and CTCF sequence/token files match their historical counterparts byte for byte, and the historical screen conclusion is reproduced. Refitted probe outputs differ slightly across the recorded environments. The clean pipeline completed through recorded resumptions after a loader compatibility repair; a diagnostic traceback timer was removed following a native Python crash. Earlier disk-exhaustion failures and all failed or interrupted attempts are preserved.

\section{Conclusion}
Calling a genomic head a biological motif detector requires connecting motif-local measurements to interventions on a validated output for the same motif. Frozen prediction and probe restoration support narrower claims. No head passed the historical motif-local QK and attention-enrichment screens, while separate sequence-level QK and native-attention assays characterize conditional genomic associations. Native CTCF causality remains unresolved.
