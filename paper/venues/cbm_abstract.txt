Interventions in genomic transformers can compare different nucleotide positions even when their activation tensors have equal shapes. MINTS audits this failure and implements explicit nucleotide-span and vocabulary correspondence for variable-length tokenization, with matched sequence controls and separate targets for trained readouts and native predictions. Six of ten historical TATA patching pairs fail correspondence. Repaired CTCF patching of a discovery-selected head changes a fixed trained readout by 0.0157 decision-score units on 64 held-out sequences in 45 genomic clusters, with a 95% block interval of [0.0066, 0.0251], while its mean base density is near the background value of one. A separately frozen mapped-query natural-variant study retains 61 cases in 60 genomic clusters. Variant and sham Jensen--Shannon divergences average 0.013053 and 0.029018 nats; the paired contrast is -0.015966, with interval [-0.024454, -0.009146]. Larger sham shifts contradict the positive differential-sensitivity hypothesis on this small eligible population and stop native head search. We formalize two limited control-feasibility obstructions and report the eligibility gained by query correspondence without claiming that it isolates segmentation effects. The contribution is a reproducible computational validity audit, not evidence of biological binding causality or generally calibrated sensitivity to learned mechanisms.
