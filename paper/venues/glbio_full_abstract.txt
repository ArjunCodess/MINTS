Motivation: Variable-length tokenization can invalidate genomic component interventions even when tensors have equal shapes. We test nucleotide correspondence and control feasibility in DNABERT-2.
Results: Six of ten historical TATA pairs fail correspondence. Repaired CTCF patching yields a 0.0157 trained-readout effect on 64 sequences in 45 clusters, with 95% block interval [0.0066, 0.0251]. TATA remains inconclusive. A separately frozen mapped-query study retains 61 natural-variant cases in 60 clusters; variant-minus-sham Jensen--Shannon divergence is -0.015966 nats, with interval [-0.024454, -0.009146]. Larger sham shifts fail the positive sensitivity gate and stop head search. Explicit query correspondence increases eligibility but leaves segmentation effects unresolved. Biological binding causality is not established.
