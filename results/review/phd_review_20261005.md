# MINTS research review, 5 October 2026

MINTS is currently strongest as an auditable case study of interpretability claims. Its main research weakness is that neither a biological motif mechanism nor the sensitivity and specificity of the proposed evidence standard has been established. Several limits are already stated accurately in the manuscript; acknowledging them does not replace the experiments needed to resolve them.

This review inspected the manuscript, references, implementation, tests, saved predictions, native-control scores, and held-out patching effects. It did not rerun the full model pipeline or independently reconstruct biological labels. The numerical diagnostics below use existing outputs and the locally cached pinned DNABERT-2 tokenizer. No implementation or manuscript was changed.

## Priority 1: experiments that determine whether the scientific interpretation holds

1. **Validate nucleotide correspondence before position-wise patching.** `src/patching.py:801` admits pairs when token tensor shapes agree, then patches by token index. Re-tokenizing the ten saved held-out TATA pairs shows that six have different nucleotide offsets at positions actually patched. For the first pair, token 5 covers bases [20,26) in the clean sequence and [20,24) after corruption. This changes the meaning of a position-wise intervention. Generate edits with compatible token boundaries, or define and validate an explicit alignment-based intervention, then rerun the experiment. Add a regression fixture with equal token counts and different boundaries.

2. **Separate motif removal from composition changes.** `src/counterfactuals.py:38` constructs a G/C-only replacement, and the TATA corruption replaces an A/T-rich motif with that sequence. Seven of ten saved pairs have negative clean-minus-corrupted probe scores, so corruption increases the positive-class decision score in most eligible examples. Compare several motif-destroying edits against non-motif edits matched for composition change, edit count, location, and tokenizer effects. Re-score every edited sequence to check motif destruction and newly created motif hits. These controls are needed before assigning the observed effects specifically to TATA identity.

3. **Connect localization and intervention for the same motif and output.** The main localization analyses concern CTCF, while patching concerns promoter and splice probes. Those experiments cannot jointly identify a CTCF circuit. Define a CTCF-specific output, validate its relationship to binding or another clearly stated task, and intervene on the same candidate components. A fine-tuned binding predictor would support claims about that predictor; a masked-language-model output would support claims about the specified sequence-modeling behavior, without automatically establishing binding biology.

4. **Replace mean normalized restoration as the primary patching endpoint.** The selected layer-2/head-7 mean PM is 0.3611, but its median is 0.0306. One pair has PM 3.2554; removing it reduces the mean to 0.03946. Mixed denominator signs also make positive restoration ratios biologically inconsistent across pairs. Make absolute decision-score changes the primary endpoint, show individual effects and leave-one-out sensitivity, and use any normalized endpoint with an explicitly fixed scale. Report direction-specific analyses as descriptive checks rather than silently selecting favorable pairs.

5. **Calibrate the evidence standard against known mechanisms.** A failed screen does not distinguish absence of a detector from an insensitive assay. Test the complete workflow on a model with an implanted or otherwise known motif detector, then on composition-only and randomized-model controls. Measure detection power and false-positive rates across motif strength, position, number of instances, and distributed implementations. This would turn a reporting discipline into a tested methodological contribution.

6. **Control tokenizer geometry in the native-attention comparison.** `tools/review_ctcf_controls.py:28` matches chromosome, GC fraction, and nucleotide length. Of the 5,220 saved pairs, 4,350 have unequal numbers of real BPE tokens, with differences as large as 13 tokens. The assay normalizes by real-key count and includes positional bias, so nucleotide-length matching alone leaves geometry differences. Compare results under token-count and target-span balance, with base-level attention summaries and positional controls. The token-count difference has a modest Spearman correlation of 0.0837 with the layer-6/head-3 contrast; this diagnoses a remaining confound to test, not an explanation of the whole signal.

7. **Make the CTCF control population answer a biological question.** Motif-absent CTCF peaks are still binding-assay peaks. This comparison estimates association with a PWM hit conditional on being a peak, rather than binding versus non-binding. Keep that contrast, but add accessible non-peak regions and motif-bearing non-peaks with comparable genomic context. Report balance in peak signal, local composition, repeat content, and accessibility where those measurements are available.

## Priority 2: inference, robustness, and contribution

8. **Separate exploratory selection from confirmation.** There is no independent validation partition, and the reported patching head is the maximum over 144 heads in ten pairs. Choose settings and components on a discovery population, freeze them, and evaluate on new chromosome-held-out or external examples. For an exploratory search, report simultaneous uncertainty or a maximum-statistic procedure rather than treating selected-head marginal intervals as discovery evidence. Expanding the eligible pair population requires addressing the current tokenizer-based selection, not pooling training examples into evaluation.

9. **Account for genomic dependence in uncertainty.** `tools/review_ctcf_controls.py:60` treats matched pairs as independent bootstrap and sign-flip units. Nearby or overlapping loci can share sequence and assay structure, so sequence-level sampling alone does not establish independent replication. Audit overlaps and homologous regions, form genomic blocks, and compare block-based intervals and null inference with the existing estimates. Report chromosome-specific effects and matching diagnostics. Matching also does not itself establish the exchangeability required for the observational sign-flip test.

10. **Measure incremental predictive information beyond simple sequence features.** GC matching changes the evaluated population, so the full-versus-matched AUROC decrease does not isolate a GC effect. Fit baseline-only and baseline-plus-residual classifiers on the same training population, select their regularization using a separate validation split, and compare them on identical held-out sequences. Include direct motif-score and position baselines. This would show what frozen representations add after accounting for the known shortcuts.

11. **Report paired differences between classifiers.** Separate classifier intervals do not directly answer whether one method improves on another. An additional exploratory paired bootstrap of saved predictions, using 2,000 sequence draws and seed 1729, gives probe-minus-k-mer AUROC differences of -0.0161 [-0.0495,0.0160] for TATA promoters, -0.0023 [-0.0094,0.0046] for other promoters, 0.0769 [0.0640,0.0902] for donors, and 0.0891 [0.0741,0.1032] for acceptors. Both promoter intervals cross zero. Add paired comparisons to the official reporting workflow and assess their sensitivity to genomic dependence and training variability.

12. **Vary the motif definition, not only the head cutoffs.** The existing sensitivity workflow thresholds saved head statistics, while the PWM support definition stays fixed. The 80%-of-score-range rule and maximum-overlap token aggregation determine which peaks enter the present/absent groups. Re-run motif scoring, matching, and inference across calibrated motif thresholds and alternate support definitions. Compare query-specific measurements with the global query average, which can dilute a detector active only at particular queries.

13. **Validate the sparse intervention against simpler intervention schemes.** The default sparse selector uses geometric blocks and known edit coordinates. It does not establish that its selected positions capture a causal pathway. Compare all-position, edit-only, flank-only, equal-budget random-position, and special-token-excluded patches, plus reverse-direction interventions. Log the exact positions and offsets for each pair. At `src/patching.py:864`, an arbitrary alignment exception silently triggers the central-token fallback; scientific runs should record and exclude failed alignments rather than substitute an intervention without notice.

14. **State and substantiate the novelty more narrowly.** `paper/references.bib` contains only three entries. The original DNABERT work already extracts motifs from attention, and prior patching work studies sensitivity to corruption and metric choices. Compare explicitly with those approaches and explain the additional testable result MINTS contributes. Useful primary sources include [DNABERT](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11025658/), [Zhang and Nanda on patching metrics and methods](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.16042), and [OmniGenBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.14402). Calibration in item 5 would strengthen this contribution substantially. A second motif and independently collected assay would test whether the standard transfers beyond this case study.

## Priority 3: implementation and reproducibility

15. **Validate dependencies when resuming a run.** The isolated runner records source hashes, but recording them does not reject incompatible upstream caches. A resumed pipeline can start after configuration writing and consume existing artifacts under the currently supplied configuration. Before each stage, verify model/tokenizer revision, relevant settings, upstream hashes, and cache membership. Reject incompatible inputs and preserve a consolidated lineage across resumptions. Add small tests for changed checkpoint, pooling, and probe-layer settings.

16. **Preserve actual layer IDs in patching tables.** `save_restoration_matrix` writes matrix row numbers as layer IDs, but batch analysis later calls `layers.index(row.layer)`. For a requested subset such as `(5,11)`, exported rows are labeled 0 and 1, causing mislabeling and a subsequent lookup failure. Pass the true layer IDs to the writer and test noncontiguous subsets. This is a code-inspection finding; the default all-layer run is unaffected.

17. **Make the documented environment independently repeatable.** The root `requirements.txt` is unpinned, while the recorded exact package list is specific to a Windows CUDA environment and model-loading compatibility patches. Provide supported platform-specific dependency constraints, a small CPU CI workflow, and an explicit environment preflight. Separate fast synthetic tests from scientific artifact-recomputation checks so a passing suite has a clear scope.
